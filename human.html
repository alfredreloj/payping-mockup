What's actually happening here

When money hits the owner's GCash, the app catches that notification, reads off the amount (and the reference number, if GCash bothered to include it), and fires it up to the store. That's the whole trick. No screenshots, no group chat, no "pa-verify po."

It reads GCash and nothing else — your texts, your bank alerts, your Messenger, all invisible to it. We were pretty firm about that; it's someone's personal phone.

The boring part that makes or breaks it

An app that reads notifications is easy. An app that's still reading them three weeks later, on a phone someone actually uses every day, is the hard part. Here's how we keep it honest.

It just runs. In the background. All day.

Install it once and leave it. You don't open it, you don't tap anything — it sits there watching for GCash even with the phone locked in a drawer.

Phones try to kill it. We plan for that.

Xiaomi, Oppo, Realme — they're ruthless about shutting down background apps to save battery, and that's the number-one way these things die quietly. Setup walks you through switching that off for PayPing, and it climbs back up on its own after a restart.

If it ever does stop, you find out fast.

The phone taps the store on the shoulder every minute. Miss a few and the register throws up a warning — because a dead reader and a slow day look exactly the same, and that's the mistake that costs you money.

Before you get the wrong idea —

This only knows about money that comes into that one GCash. Cash, cards, GrabPay, your other e-wallet — not here, that's your POS's job. And it's Android only. Apple flat-out won't let apps read notifications, so an iPhone is a non-starter. Nothing we can do about that one, sorry.
